Designed and modeled a carbon fiber leaf spring landing gear assembly in SolidWorks, individually developing the curved leaf spring, wheel clamps, axles, wheels, fuselage mounting brackets, and fastening hardware. The leaf spring features an 18.78-inch span, 7.25-inch overall height, 1.15-inch width, and 0.35-inch thickness. Designed the assembly using 6061-T6 aluminum mounting components, alloy steel fasteners, and a carbon fiber composite spring to balance structural strength, weight, and manufacturability. Incorporated custom mounting brackets, bolted connections, and lightening pockets to create a realistic aircraft landing gear system.

Performed hand calculations to evaluate the structural behavior of the leaf spring under an assumed 165 lbf (734 N) landing load, distributed equally between the two wheel contact points at approximately 82.5 lbf (367 N) per wheel. Developed a free-body diagram and calculated support reactions, shear forces, bending moments, and theoretical bending stresses. Although the spring features a curved geometry, classical straight-beam bending equations were used as a first-order approximation because the radius of curvature was sufficiently large relative to the beam depth (R/h > 10). This allowed the spring to be approximated as a slender beam for preliminary bending analysis, while recognizing that curvature and axial forces could influence the actual stress distribution. Constructed shear force and bending moment diagrams to visualize internal loading and identify critical regions.

Conducted finite element analysis (FEA) in SolidWorks Simulation to evaluate structural performance under the assumed landing load. The initial spring analysis produced a maximum von Mises stress of approximately 64.9 MPa and maximum displacement of 1.21 mm. Examined first principal stresses, stress concentrations, and deformation patterns to assess the spring's structural response. Extended the analysis to the landing gear assembly, incorporating mounting brackets, wheel clamps, and fastening hardware to investigate load transfer between components. Compared theoretical beam calculations with FEA results to evaluate the effects of curved geometry, material behavior, loading conditions, and boundary constraints.

This project demonstrates the integration of detailed 3D CAD modeling, mechanical assembly design, classical beam theory, hand calculations, and finite element analysis in the development and structural evaluation of an aircraft landing gear system.
