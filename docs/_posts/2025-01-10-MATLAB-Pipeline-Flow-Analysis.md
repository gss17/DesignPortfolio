Developed a MATLAB-based engineering model to analyze fluid flow and pressure losses in a pipeline system, applying core fluid mechanics principles to a real-world energy transport scenario. The model used the Darcy–Weisbach equation to evaluate how key design parameters—including pipe length, diameter, and flow rate—impact system performance and efficiency. Through simulation and visualization of pressure behavior, the project highlights important trade-offs in pipeline design, demonstrating how increased flow rates and longer distances lead to higher pressure losses, while larger pipe diameters reduce resistance. This work reflects an interest in applying mechanical design and analysis to energy systems, particularly within oil and gas transport.
