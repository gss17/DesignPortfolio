Designed a detailed retractable trailing-link aircraft landing gear assembly in SolidWorks, incorporating a main structural strut, dual-wheel configuration, trailing arm, shock absorber, upper and lower drag braces, and a linear retraction actuator. Individually modeled the primary mechanical components, including the landing gear mounting structure, pivot connections, linkage arms, and supporting hardware. The trailing-link configuration allows the wheels to rotate about the lower pivot during landing, compressing the shock absorber to absorb impact loads. The upper and lower drag braces provide structural support and guide the landing gear through its retraction sequence, while the actuator is designed to control the folding mechanism.

Developed SolidWorks motion studies to evaluate the assembly's kinematics and demonstrate the transition between fully extended and retracted configurations. Verified the rotational movement of the landing gear about its primary mounting pivot and examined the coordinated motion of the drag-brace linkages throughout retraction. The design also separates landing-impact suspension movement from the retraction mechanism, allowing the trailing arm and shock absorber to operate independently of the gear's overall rotation. The project demonstrates mechanical design, assembly modeling, linkage development, and motion simulation, with an emphasis on creating a realistic aircraft landing gear system.
